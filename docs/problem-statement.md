# Problem Statement

## Background
Electrical power grids are the backbone of modern society and industrial manufacturing. Within this infrastructure, high-voltage substations, step-down transformers, and distribution circuit breakers operate continuously under extreme electrical and thermal stress.

## The Problem
Power utility operations and facility engineering teams currently face high operational risk due to:
1. **Unpredicted Catastrophic Equipment Failures:** Transformers frequently experience catastrophic failures from overheating, internal dielectric breakdown, and partial discharge without sufficient early warning.
2. **Weather-Induced Cascading Outages:** Extreme meteorological events (thunderstorms, high wind gusts, heatwaves) cause sudden feeder trips, leading to widespread blackout conditions affecting thousands of industrial customers.
3. **Fragmented SCADA Telemetry:** Sensor data (dissolved gas analysis, oil quality, core temperatures, vibration, voltage fluctuations) are locked in siloed monitoring tools with no real-time AI risk synthesis or automated crew dispatching.

## Who is Affected
- **Power Utility Grid Operators & Dispatchers:** Managing regional power distribution networks and substation reliability.
- **Industrial Facility & Plant Managers:** Requiring uninterrupted high-voltage power feeds for continuous manufacturing lines.
- **Field Maintenance & Engineering Crews:** Responsible for rapid emergency restoration and scheduled preventative servicing.

## Why It Matters
A single transformer breakdown can cause hours of unscheduled blackout, hundreds of thousands of dollars in lost manufacturing output, equipment replacement costs exceeding $1M+, and severe grid stability hazards. Proactive failure prediction and automated downtime estimation can reduce catastrophic failure incidents by over 60%.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short
Traditional utility operations rely on periodic manual oil sampling (every 6–12 months) and static threshold alarms that only trigger when an asset is already in a critical state. They lack dynamic machine learning inference on Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA) profiles, combined meteorological risk correlation, and explainable multi-factor attribution.
